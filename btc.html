Four timeframes, one engine. Daily: ~60-day cycle (40–75d) with the ~22-week weekly nested on top. 4-Hour: ~8-day cycle (36–72 bars) under the daily cycle. 1-Hour: ~2-day cycle (30–72 bars) under the ~8-day 4H cycle. 5-Min: ~3-hour cycle (24–60 bars) under a ~12-hour half-day cycle. Cycles are counted low-to-low; from each confirmed low a timing window opens and the lowest price inside it becomes the next low, with smoothing to ignore single-bar noise and a "chase" that follows a still-falling low past its window. History: Daily pages back to the 2017 listing (~9 years), 4-Hour ≈ 500 days, 1-Hour ≈ 42 days, 5-Min ≈ 3.5 days; cycle lengths are starting estimates — tune them in each CFG block. The cycle-low markers are hindsight labels (the smoother sees ±2 bars, chased lows confirm ~8 bars late, and the newest low stays provisional) — good for measuring cycles, never for entering on. The tradable layer is the causal Cycle-confluence strategy in the backtester.
iMACD. Each tab's impulse pane (LazyBear 34/9) is computed on that tab's candles. Threshold = the 55th-percentile crossover size (the major/minor split); dots mark impulse turns (green up, red down), zigzag-filtered to ignore noise. The amber band projects the next cycle-low timing window.
RSI & divergences. Each tab's RSI pane is a 14-period Wilder's Relative Strength Index on that tab's closes, bounded 0–100 (70 overbought, 30 oversold, 50 midline; live reading in the header). Arrows mark confirmed divergences between consecutive price pivots (±3-bar extremes, so each is only known 3 bars after the pivot — a fixed, disclosed lag, no repainting): green up = price lower low with RSI higher low from an oversold first pivot ("DIV" = RSI still <40 at the second, the stronger class); red down = the bearish mirror. The iMACD pane carries the same detector against the impulse line. All three panes share one time axis.
Backtester. Two strategies: iMACD cross (crossover below the oversold threshold, fixed TP/SL) and Cycle confluence (window-gated, divergence-scored entries with structural stops at the candidate low, +1.5R scale-out, pivot trailing, and translation/age exits). Both are fully causal — thresholds are expanding percentiles, pivots respect their confirmation lag — and net of the fee input. The optimizer is walk-forward: fitted on the first 70% of history, judged on the untouched last 30%; only the test columns mean anything. Signal journal below appends a row when a capture pass (page load, Refresh, or the automatic 5-minute recheck) finds the causal confluence trigger fired on the newest closed bar — a forward record written in localStorage that never repaints, though fast-tab triggers can slip between rechecks and captures only happen while the page is open.
Funding gauge. The header chip reads the BTC/USDT perpetual funding rate (Binance, falling back to Bybit) and its percentile against its own trailing history — deeply negative funding at a low (p≤10, shown in green) means short-heavy perps are paying longs, a forced-seller capitulation tell; crowded-long euphoria (p≥90, red) is the mirror. This is a live-only read: funding history only reaches back ~333 days on the primary source, far short of the price history used elsewhere, so the score card's funding component is scored on the current bar only and is excluded from all backtests and the walk-forward optimizer to avoid silently truncating the sample to a cherry-picked recent window.
Read it as structure, not prophecy. Timing windows are wide and the most recent low stays provisional until price turns up, so the right edge can revise. This is a discretionary orientation and risk frame (a broken cycle low is a natural invalidation), not a forecast. A 2026-07 instrumented audit (score-gate sweeps, exit ablations, forward returns of every confirmed low vs an unconditional control, walk-forward tests) found no demonstrated net edge at the default score≥2 gate on Daily or 4-Hour — samples are tiny, two score components ran inverse to forward returns, and every optimizer pick decayed out-of-sample. Treat the signal card as decision support and let the journal accumulate forward evidence before trusting any configuration. Not investment advice.
