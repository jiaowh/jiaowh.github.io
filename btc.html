Four timeframes, one engine. Daily: ~60-day cycle (40–75d) with the ~22-week weekly nested on top. 4-Hour: ~8-day cycle (36–72 bars) under the daily cycle. 1-Hour: ~2-day cycle (30–72 bars) under the ~8-day 4H cycle. 5-Min: ~3-hour cycle (24–60 bars) under a ~12-hour half-day cycle. Cycles are counted low-to-low; from each confirmed low a timing window opens and the lowest price inside it becomes the next low, with smoothing to ignore single-bar noise and a "chase" that follows a still-falling low past its window. History: Daily pages back to the 2017 listing (~9 years), 4-Hour ≈ 500 days, 1-Hour ≈ 42 days, 5-Min ≈ 3.5 days; cycle lengths are starting estimates — tune them in each CFG block. The cycle-low markers are hindsight labels (the smoother sees ±2 bars, chased lows confirm ~8 bars late, and the newest low stays provisional) — good for measuring cycles, never for entering on. The tradable layer is the causal signal geometry drawn on the charts.
iMACD. Each tab's impulse pane (LazyBear 34/9) is computed on that tab's candles. Threshold = the 55th-percentile crossover size (the major/minor split); dots mark impulse turns (green up, red down), zigzag-filtered to ignore noise. The amber band projects the next cycle-low timing window.
RSI & divergences. Each tab's RSI pane is a 14-period Wilder's Relative Strength Index on that tab's closes, bounded 0–100 (70 overbought, 30 oversold, 50 midline; live reading in the header). Arrows mark confirmed divergences between consecutive price pivots (±3-bar extremes, so each is only known 3 bars after the pivot — a fixed, disclosed lag, no repainting): green up = price lower low with RSI higher low from an oversold first pivot ("DIV" = RSI still <40 at the second, the stronger class); red down = the bearish mirror. The iMACD pane carries the same detector against the impulse line. All three panes share one time axis.
How to read. Entries. Price above its green SMA200 (no red tint), cycle age inside the amber window, then a bar closing above the amber TRIG line prints a green trg arrow on that bar, with a dim green dot back at the low it confirmed — the pair shows the confirmation lag, and neither mark ever moves. Every confirmed low gets the same arrow: the forward audit found the confluence score did not rank lows, so the regime tint, not the marker, decides whether a trigger is worth taking. If price undercuts a confirmed low before the next timing window opens, the count re-anchors to the new low rather than holding a level price has already broken — so the arrows track the live count, unlike the dimmed DCL labels, which quietly relocate once the whole window is in hindsight. Exits. The amber Donchian-20 rail is the trailing exit: leave on a close below it. It starts at the last confirmed low and ratchets up as the trade advances, never falling. There is no time exit — the exit ablations measured ~49-bar holds once the rail was allowed to run. BB dots are a separate sleeve, not a cycle trade — a close under the lower Bollinger band while still above SMA200, covered at the mid band. Arrows printed under the red tint (price below SMA200) are knife-catches; that is the one bucket the audit showed losing money, so the regime shading is the filter to respect.
Funding gauge. The header chip reads the BTC/USDT perpetual funding rate (Binance, falling back to Bybit) and its percentile against its own trailing history — deeply negative funding at a low (p≤10, shown in green) means short-heavy perps are paying longs, a forced-seller capitulation tell; crowded-long euphoria (p≥90, red) is the mirror. This is a live-only read: funding history only reaches back ~333 days on the primary source, far short of the price history used elsewhere, so the score card's funding component is scored on the current bar only and is excluded from all backtests and the walk-forward optimizer to avoid silently truncating the sample to a cherry-picked recent window.
Liq Map. The liquidation heatmap is an estimate, not exchange data. Each perpetual-futures candle's taker buy volume is modeled as new longs and taker sell volume as new shorts, projected to the prices where 10×, 25×, 50× and 100× positions would be force-closed — long liquidations stack below price, shorts above. Levels accumulate, fade slowly, and are consumed the first time price trades through them, so the surviving bright bands mark dense, still-untouched stop pools that tend to act as magnets and as fuel for wicks. Colors run dark → blue → cyan → green → yellow as modeled density grows (log-scaled and top-weighted, so yellow marks only the very densest pools); the profile on the right shows the current un-swept book per price level (short liquidations green above price, long liquidations orange below) with a cumulative curve running outward from price and each side’s dollar total at the top and bottom; the readout under the map names the largest un-swept cluster on each side, and hovering reads any cell or profile level in dollars. Dollar figures assume ~5% of taker volume opens fresh leveraged positions — an order-of-magnitude calibration, not a measurement. Real liquidation engines see actual position data — this model cannot — so treat the map as crowd-positioning context to pair with the funding chip, not as levels anyone is guaranteed to defend.
Read it as structure, not prophecy. Timing windows are wide and the most recent low stays provisional until price turns up, so the right edge can revise. This is a discretionary orientation and risk frame (a broken cycle low is a natural invalidation), not a forecast. A 2026-07 instrumented audit (score-gate sweeps, exit ablations, forward returns of every confirmed low vs an unconditional control, walk-forward tests) found no demonstrated net edge at the default score≥2 gate on Daily or 4-Hour — samples are tiny, two score components ran inverse to forward returns, and every optimizer pick decayed out-of-sample. Treat the signal card as decision support and let the journal accumulate forward evidence before trusting any configuration. Not investment advice.
